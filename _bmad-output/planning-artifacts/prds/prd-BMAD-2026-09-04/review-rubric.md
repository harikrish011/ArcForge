# PRD Quality Review — Parking Spot Finder

## Overall verdict
This is a well-earned PRD: FRs carry testable consequences almost without exception, the Non-Goals/Assumption/NOTE-FOR-PM discipline is used at real tensions rather than as decoration, and the launch-grade-stakes-vs-one-day-build tension the user flagged is handled honestly rather than smoothed over — the aspirational NFRs (accessibility, safety) are actually operationalized into concrete FR-level behavior (badge non-color-only, one-tap detail/route) rather than left as unactionable adjectives. What's at risk is mechanical, not structural: the two Success Metrics carry no actual target number (the `[ASSUMPTION]` tag stands in for the whole figure, not a value), the Assumptions Index doesn't fully round-trip against inline tags, and there's an unreconciled tension between the "no user-adjustable radius" Non-Goal and FR-6's assumed one-tap radius-expansion behavior. None of these require rework — they're fill-in-the-number and cross-reference fixes.

## Decision-readiness — strong
Decisions read as decisions: MVP scope (§6.1/6.2) states FR-1–8 as Must Have and FR-9/10 as "cut first under schedule pressure" rather than burying the cut. Open Questions (§8) are genuinely unresolved — OQ-4 ("whether this product continues past the hackathon") is a real fork with stated downstream consequences ("informs whether 'web only, fixed radius, no accounts' are permanent decisions or one-day cuts"), not a rhetorical question answered in the next line. `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts land on real tensions: the Cross-Cutting NFRs accessibility note and the Safety constraint note both name a genuine conflict (Launch-grade stakes vs. one-day build) rather than parking at a safe checkpoint.

No findings — this dimension does its job.

## Substance over theater — strong
Two personas (Ritu, Arjun), each driving a distinct FR set (UJ-1 → FR-1,3,4,5,7,8; UJ-2 → FR-2,6) — not persona theater. The Vision (§1) is specific enough to resist being swapped into a generic parking-app PRD: it names what's deliberately *not* built (live occupancy, reservations, payments) as part of the value proposition, not just a features list. NFRs mostly carry thresholds rather than boilerplate adjectives — "500ms," "distinguishable by more than color," "keyboard operable," a named evergreen-browser list.

### Findings
- **medium** No explicit Security NFR (§Cross-Cutting NFRs) — Given the user's explicit Launch-grade stakes choice, Cross-Cutting NFRs covers Performance, Accessibility, Availability, Browser support, and Privacy, but nothing names a security stance for the one live surface the product has: the `GET /parking/nearby` endpoint. FR-3 does reject malformed params with a 4xx (a de facto security behavior), and the addendum's guardrail content is rightly scoped out as process methodology — but the *product's* own security posture (e.g., HTTPS-only, no secrets in the client bundle) isn't stated anywhere in the PRD itself. The risk surface is small (no auth, no accounts, no PII), which is why this is medium rather than high. *Fix:* Add a one-line Security NFR bullet, e.g. "Served over HTTPS; no credentials or API keys in client-side code; malformed query params rejected (ties FR-3)."

## Strategic coherence — strong
The thesis is explicit and load-bearing: "a fast, honest answer" (§1), operationalized as the Free/Paid badge being a trust signal the product exists to protect. Feature prioritization follows from it — FR-9/10 (filter/sort) are correctly the first things cut, since they refine rather than deliver the core honesty/speed promise. The counter-metric is a genuine strength: SM-C1 explicitly forbids optimizing SM-1 (speed-to-route) by degrading classification confidence — a real thesis-protecting mechanism, not a pro-forma "counter-metrics" section.

### Findings
- **medium** Success Metrics have no numeric target (§7) — SM-1 reads "`[ASSUMPTION]` % of sessions where the user reaches Route Handoff... within 60 seconds," and SM-2 similarly — the `[ASSUMPTION]` tag stands in for the entire percentage, not a specific assumed value. Contrast with how `[ASSUMPTION]` is used elsewhere in the PRD (e.g. FR-4: "`[ASSUMPTION: rounded to nearest 0.1km]`" — a concrete stand-in value flagged as assumed). As written, SM-1/SM-2 aren't falsifiable — there's no bar to compare a demo-day result against. *Fix:* Pick a placeholder number consistent with how other assumptions are written, e.g. "`[ASSUMPTION: 70%]` of sessions reach Route Handoff within 60 seconds," so the metric is at least testable even if the number itself is later revised.

## Done-ness clarity — strong
This is the PRD's best dimension. FR-1 through FR-8 each carry consequences with actual bounds: exact query contract and rejection behavior (FR-3: "`GET /parking/nearby?lat&lng&radius`... Out-of-range or malformed... rejected with a 4xx response rather than silently defaulting"), explicit degradation paths (FR-5: "the app degrades to list-only view (FR-4) rather than blocking the user"), explicit field-omission rules (FR-7: "missing fields are omitted rather than rendered as blank placeholders"). No instances of "handles gracefully" or "reasonable performance" without a bound attached. An engineer could write acceptance tests directly off most of these consequences without further clarification.

### Findings
- **medium** Non-Goal vs. FR-6 tension on radius adjustability — §5 states "No user-adjustable search radius in v1 — fixed at 1km," and FR-3's Out of Scope line repeats "User-adjustable radius (Non-Goal, v1)." But FR-6's consequences assume "`[ASSUMPTION: a one-tap expansion, e.g. to 2km]`" when zero results are found — which is a form of radius adjustment, even if system-offered rather than a free slider. The PRD doesn't reconcile these: an engineer implementing FR-6 can't tell from the text alone whether the one-tap expansion violates the "fixed at 1km" Non-Goal or is understood as a distinct, narrower escape hatch. *Fix:* Add a clause to either §5 or FR-6 clarifying the distinction, e.g. "Non-Goal covers user-driven arbitrary radius control (slider/input); the one-tap zero-result expansion (FR-6) is a single system-offered step, not general adjustability."

## Scope honesty — strong
Non-Goals (§5) is a real, itemized list, not a gesture — nine explicit exclusions with implicit rationale (infrastructure the MVP doesn't have). `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` tags on FR-9/10 correctly distinguish "cut under time pressure, might come back" from the harder §5 Non-Goals. De-scoping is proposed honestly: §6.2 states FR-9/10 as "cut first under time pressure" with a `[NOTE FOR PM: revisit if build stays ahead of schedule]` rather than silently dropping them. Open-item density (4 Open Questions, 6 Assumptions Index entries, several inline `[NOTE FOR PM]`) is proportionate given the PRD's own explicit framing of the Launch-grade/one-day tension — high enough to be honest, not so high it reads as unfinished.

### Findings
- **medium** Assumptions Index doesn't fully round-trip (§9 vs. inline tags) — Three inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags have no corresponding §9 entry: Cross-Cutting NFRs → Performance ("`[ASSUMPTION]` ...responds within 500ms"), Browser support ("`[ASSUMPTION]` Latest evergreen browsers..."), and Constraints → Cost ("...no paid infrastructure spend is assumed for the MVP. `[ASSUMPTION]`"). Conversely, §9's last bullet cites "SM-1, SM-2, SM-C1" as assumed, but SM-C1's text (§7) carries no inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tag of its own — the index over-claims there. The same §9 bullet also mislocates the Safety assumption as "§Cross-Cutting NFRs" when it's actually under "§Constraints and Guardrails › Safety." *Fix:* Add index entries for the Performance, Browser support, and Cost assumptions; drop or add a tag to reconcile the SM-C1 mention; correct the Safety assumption's section reference.

## Downstream usability — strong
Glossary (§3) covers the 8 terms that matter and they're used consistently in capitalized form across FRs and UJs (Active Location, Parking Spot, Spot Detail Panel). FR IDs (1–10), UJ IDs (1–2), and SM IDs (1, 2, C1) are contiguous with no gaps or duplicates. Every FR names which UJ(s) it realizes, and cross-references resolve (e.g. FR-4's "per the accessibility NFR in §Cross-Cutting NFRs" points at an actual heading). This PRD is chain-top per its own Doc Purpose (feeds "downstream architecture/epics workflows") and the addendum's Technical-How is explicitly framed as "input to bmad-architecture," so this dimension carries real weight here — and it holds up.

### Findings
- **low** FR-9's UJ-1 realization is a stretch — FR-9 ("Realizes UJ-1") claims to serve Ritu's journey, but UJ-1's narrated path (§2.3) never touches filtering — she goes straight from list+map to the free spot to Route. This isn't wrong so much as unproven; UJ-1's written path doesn't visibly exercise the FR that claims it. *Fix:* Either add a filter-touching branch to UJ-1's path, or soften the FR-9 claim to describe the general use case it serves rather than "Realizes UJ-1."

## Shape fit — strong
The product is a consumer-facing tool with meaningful UX, and the PRD's shape matches: two load-bearing, named-protagonist UJs (not persona-theater padding), FRs with testable consequences, a proportionate NFR/Constraints section. The Launch-grade stakes choice explains the fuller formalization (Glossary, Cross-Cutting NFRs, Constraints and Guardrails, IA) relative to what a bare hackathon README would carry — and critically, that formalization isn't hollow: the accessibility and safety NFRs that the PRD itself flags as tension-prone against a one-day build are translated into concrete, already-achievable FR behavior (badge non-color-only in FR-4/FR-5; one-tap reachability in FR-7/FR-8) rather than left as aspirational prose. No over- or under-formalization to flag.

## Mechanical notes
- **Glossary case drift** — "Radius" is capitalized as a defined term in body text (§4.2 FR-3: "within a 1km Radius of the Active Location") but appears lowercase in section/bullet headers ("Nearby parking query (1km radius)" in the FR-3 title; "No user-adjustable search radius in v1" in §5). Meaning is unambiguous throughout; this is cosmetic.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip gaps** — see the Scope honesty finding above for detail: three inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (Performance, Browser support, Cost) are missing from §9; SM-C1 is cited in §9 without a matching inline tag; the Safety assumption's §9 location reference names the wrong section.
- **ID continuity** — clean. FR-1–10, UJ-1–2, SM-1/2/C1 all contiguous, unique, no dangling cross-references found.
